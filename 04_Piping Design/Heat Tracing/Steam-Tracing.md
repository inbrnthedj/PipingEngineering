# Steam Tracing in Process Plants

## What is Steam Tracing?
Steam tracing is a method used to maintain or raise the temperature of pipes, valves, and vessels in a process plant. It involves running one or more smaller-diameter tubes or pipes (carrying steam) parallel to the main process line. Both the process line and the tracer are then wrapped together in thermal insulation. Heat transfers from the steam tracer to the process fluid, preventing it from freezing, solidifying, or dropping below its dew point.

## Where is it Used?
Steam tracing is primarily used in plants where fluids are highly viscous, temperature-sensitive, or prone to crystallization at ambient temperatures. 
*   **Sectors:** Oil & Gas (Refineries), Biorefineries, Petrochemical, Chemical, Pharmaceuticals, and Food & Beverage.
*   **Typical Applications:** Maintaining pumpability of heavy oils/waxes in biorefineries, preventing water lines from freezing in winter, and keeping sulfur or asphalt in a liquid state.

## Materials Used for Tracing Lines
Tracer lines must be highly conductive and easy to route alongside complex piping geometry.
1.  **Copper Tubing:** Very common for low-pressure tracing. It is highly conductive, easy to bend, and quick to install, but cannot withstand corrosive environments or very high pressures.
2.  **Stainless Steel Tubing:** Used in corrosive environments or when higher temperatures and pressures are required. It is harder to bend than copper but offers superior durability.
3.  **Carbon Steel Pipe:** Often used for the main steam headers and condensate return manifolds, but less commonly for the tracing itself due to its rigidity and susceptibility to exterior corrosion under insulation (CUI).

## Steam Utility Grades (Pressure & Temperature)
Process plants typically generate and distribute steam at different energy grades depending on the heating requirement. While exact ranges vary by plant standards, a common classification is:
*   **Low-Pressure (LP) Steam:** ~150 - 180°C. Typically used for standard winterization, tracing water lines, or heating low-temp organics.
*   **Medium-Pressure (MP) Steam:** ~200 - 270°C. Used for heavier process fluids, reboilers, and driving smaller mechanical equipment.
*   **High-Pressure (HP) Steam:** ~350 - 400°C. Rarely used for simple tracing because it is too hot and valuable; primarily used for power generation (Steam Turbines) or heavy reactor heating.

## Critical Equipment: Steam Traps and Drip Legs
When steam transfers heat to the process pipe, it condenses back into water. This water must be removed immediately.
*   **Steam Traps (Scaricatori di condensa):** Mechanical valves that automatically open to discharge condensate and close to trap live steam. 
*   **Drip Legs (Condensini):** Collection pots located at low points in the steam headers or right before critical equipment like **Steam Turbines**. They catch the condensate so the steam trap can drain it, preventing "water hammer" and severe erosion of turbine blades.
