# Loads

Pipes must handle different loads:

1. Static
    - **Primary loads** 
        - non-self-limiting and can cause catastrophic failure if exceeded
        - like the constant weight of the bridge and cars — they include the weight of the pipe itself, the fluid inside, and pressure pushing from inside or outside. These loads are always there during the pipe’s life.
        - Sustained loads like weight of pipe, fluid, fittings, internal and external pressure
    - **Secondary loads**
        - self-limiting and can cause fatigue failure due to cycling
        - happen because pipes expand or contract when temperatures change, like how metal stretches in the heat. These loads can cause the pipe to bend or move.
        - Cyclic loads mainly from thermal expansion/contraction, friction at supports, and equipment settlement.
2. **Dynamic loads**: Occasional loads and must be considered for transient events.
    - Examples:
        - Wind
        - Seismic Activity
        - Vibration
        - Water hammer
        - fluid discharge forces


If any of these loads are not considered during design, the pipe might fail suddenly (catastrophic failure) or wear out over time due to repeated stress (fatigue failure).


## Identification of Loads
- Analyze the operating conditions, environment, and pipe function.
- Consider all phases: erection, testing, startup, normal operation, emergency, and maintenance.
- Identify sources of loads such as weight, pressure, temperature changes, external forces, and dynamic events.

## Failure

### Catastrophic Failure
- Happens suddenly and in a single event.
- Occurs when the pipe is subjected to a load much higher than it was designed for.
- The pipe breaks or bursts immediately without warning.
- Example: A pipe bursting during a hydraulic test because the pressure exceeded design limits.

#### How to prevent Catastrophic Failure
- Design pipes to handle all expected loads, including maximum operating pressure and test pressures (e.g., hydraulic test pressure).
- Account for all possible loads during design: weight, pressure, thermal effects, and occasional loads.
- Use appropriate materials and wall thickness based on standards like ASME B31.3.
- Ensure supports and anchors are properly designed to avoid unexpected stresses.


### Fatigue Failure
- Develops gradually over time due to repeated or cyclic loading.
- Even if each load cycle is within design limits, the repeated stress causes cracks to form and grow.
- Eventually, the pipe fails after many cycles.
- Example: Cracks forming in a pipe due to repeated thermal expansion and contraction during start-up and shutdown cycles.

#### How to prevent Catastrophic Failure
- Allow for pipe flexibility to accommodate thermal expansion and contraction using expansion joints, loops, or flexible supports.
- Avoid rigid restraints that cause high cyclic stresses.
- Consider the number of load cycles the pipe will experience and design for fatigue life accordingly.
- Use stress analysis software to simulate cyclic loads and identify critical areas.
- Regularly inspect and maintain piping systems to detect early signs of fatigue.
